# D&AD copy principles — attributed synthesis

This is a project-use synthesis inspired by D&AD's *The Copy Book* and related D&AD interviews and training articles. The book collects different practitioners' methods; it does not present one official formula. These notes are neither a verbatim reproduction nor an exhaustive summary.

## Working principles

1. Start with product truth and human tension, not a turn of phrase.
2. Decide the one idea or opinion before polishing sentences.
3. Lead with the user's benefit rather than a capability list or competitor attack.
4. Write like people speak. Prefer plain, familiar words that serve the argument.
5. Make headline and visual express one thought together.
6. Use specific facts and emotionally true observations to persuade.
7. Generate many directions before selecting; first thoughts are inputs, not conclusions.
8. Separate creator and editor. Build freely, then cut ruthlessly.
9. Remove ego and cleverness when they make the message harder to understand.
10. Know when not to write. Restraint can make the product proof stronger.
11. Fit the words to the audience, medium, journey stage, and desired action.
12. Audit attention, truth, clarity, benefit, distinctiveness, action, and trust.

## AmbitionBox translation

- Begin with a jobseeker tension the product can actually resolve: missing a recruiter moment, applying without company truth, or spending effort on a weak-fit role.
- Show the consequence through a native product artifact whenever possible.
- Use emotional directness in the headline, then calm specificity in the explanation.
- Keep trust and evidence close to the permission or claim they qualify.
- Treat a CTA as part of the proposition, not a generic continuation control.
