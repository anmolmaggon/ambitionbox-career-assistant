# Copy

## Voice

Write for an ambitious jobseeker who wants to be intentional, informed, and prepared. Use emotional directness for the promise and calm specificity for the product.

- Speak as **you** and **your**.
- Prefer short, active, concrete sentences.
- Make controls say what happens next.
- Lead with the outcome; explain evidence and limitations nearby.
- Use familiar jobseeker language, not internal system language.
- Keep one thought per label, heading, or supporting line.

## Product language

- Use **AmbitionBox** with a capital A and B.
- Keep onboarding focused on the **job search**.
- Describe intentional selection, not indiscriminate opportunity volume.
- “The right opportunity,” “what fits,” “what is real,” and “your next move” are useful territories.
- Explain that Naukri can autofill profile, résumé, and preferences, followed by review.
- Never imply that Google or Apple sign-in connects an inbox.
- Describe email as **the email you use to apply**, with Gmail as one provider.

## Current first-open pitch

The implementation currently uses three connected proof points without a separate visible headline:

> See the roles worth your time.  
> Know the pay, culture, and reality inside.  
> Get guidance from résumé to negotiation.

This is draft copy. The points carry the match → truth → guided action story themselves; do not repeat that story in a headline or adjacent reassurance copy.

The first-open Naukri action may use **⚡ Autofill your profile, résumé, and preferences.** as a compact benefit badge. Autofill is the differentiating benefit, not generic sign-in reassurance.

## CTA rules

- Use one dominant action per screen.
- Prefer natural verb-led labels: **Continue with Naukri**, **Confirm profile**, **Confirm preferences**, **Show my next move**.
- Avoid vague labels such as **Continue** when a more specific next action fits.
- Keep action vocabulary stable through loading and success states.
- Do not add decorative icons to a CTA unless the icon identifies a provider or clarifies navigation.
- On review screens, label the action being completed rather than the next screen being opened. Use **Confirm profile**, not **Review job preferences** or **Looks good**.
- Profile Review may celebrate the real autofill outcome—**Your profile, autofilled in seconds**—while immediately asking the user to review. Do not replace that factual outcome with a score or an unexplained “smart” claim.
- On imported Preferences, lead with the ranking outcome: **Let’s make sure the right jobs rise first.** Explain that the preferences shape what rises, not what the user may explore or apply to. Attribute the surface as **Your job preferences from Naukri** with **Review or change them anytime**, and complete the current action with **Confirm preferences**.
- On Profile Review, make the user consequence explicit in one concise supporting sentence: the profile powers better matches, gap checks, and tailored guidance. Do not add a second benefits list above the review surface.
- On Curation, cycle through **Curating jobs worth your time.**, **Checking what actually fits you.**, and **Bringing your strongest matches together.** Use the supporting lines to name Naukri, LinkedIn, company career pages, and leading job boards; explain that role, pay, location, work mode, and reviewed preferences shape fit; then end on focused effort and the user's best shot. Do not repeat these ideas in a checklist.

## Trust language

- Put source and privacy boundaries close to the claim or permission they qualify.
- Say what access does and does not do.
- Keep simulation notes out of the emotional hierarchy while retaining them where the demo could otherwise mislead.
- Never claim a message or application was sent if the product only drafted it.

## Current unified email pitch

Use one light screen for value, proof, provider choice, trust, and optionality. The current draft:

> Connect your inbox. We’ll take it from there.

Explain the object with:

> The inbox where your job updates already land.

The headline frames the exchange — connect once, AmbitionBox handles the rest. The prior “Never miss what happens next” tested as vague: users could not tell what connecting their inbox would give them. “We’ll take it from there” is confident and broad; the proof cases below immediately substantiate it.

Set **Connect your inbox.** in the AmbitionBox primary colour so the emphasis falls on the ask. The subcopy names the object plainly — which inbox — without repeating the proof cases or defusing a fear.

Ground the promise in six generic capability statements covering the full job search chronology: **Application tracking across every source**, **Smart follow-ups when things go quiet**, **Recruiter replies handled for you**, **Interview prep tailored to the role**, **Offer analysis with verified pay data**, and **Negotiation guidance when it counts**. Each stands alone with an icon — no connecting line, no checkmarks, no scenario-specific “trigger → action” format.

Use **Connect Gmail** with the Gmail mark and **Connect another email** as the two provider actions. Put **Skip** in the top-right of the flow header. Keep the trust boundary to one quiet line below both actions: **Read-only access. You’re in control.** Do not claim that sending is impossible forever; the product contract is explicit review before any send, while this deterministic demo never sends a real message.

These are idea territories, not approved synonyms. Keep only one on-screen.

## Account sheet, scan, and import result

Keep one vocabulary across the connection beat. The sheet repeats the ask — **Use the inbox where your job updates already land.** — and bounds it with **Read-only scan of the last 90 days.** The demo-simulation note stays; it discloses something different from the permission posture and must not be merged away.

The scan's first stage states the boundary rather than the timespan: **Only your job-related email—nothing else.** The stage stat carries the span. Do not repeat a number that another element on the same screen already owns.

The import result states meaning and lets the stat row own the counts: **Every application you sent is here, and the ones that need you are marked.**

It closes with a receipt of the four scan stages — **Scanned the last 90 days · Read your status updates · Found recruiter activity · Built your live Home** — stated as completed work. Receipt labels never carry counts.

## Avoid

- “Career Assistant” or “Career Copilot” during onboarding
- Broad claims about the user's entire career
- Generic phrases such as “the full picture,” “unlock your potential,” or “every opportunity”
- Third-person personalization such as “his preferences”
- AI theatre, unexplained intelligence, or claims without a visible source
- Repeating the same company, status, or personalization context in adjacent elements
